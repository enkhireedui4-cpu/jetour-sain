# JETOUR Mongolia — UX/UI Audit (Mobile-First)

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Scope:** `/`, `/models`, `/models/[id]`, `/test-drive`, navigation, footer, forms, image & performance strategy
**Method:** source review + live measurement in a 390×844 viewport (iPhone 14 Pro class) on the running dev server, plus asset inspection on disk.

A note on confidence: findings marked **[VERIFIED]** were measured in the browser or on the filesystem, and the measurement is quoted. Findings marked **[REVIEW]** come from reading the code and are high-confidence but were not reproduced live. Impact ratings are *engineering estimates* to help prioritisation — they are not measured conversion data and should be validated post-launch.

---

## 0. Executive summary

The foundation here is genuinely better than most dealer sites: there is a real token system in `globals.css` (one accent, one font, a fluid type scale, motion tokens, `prefers-reduced-motion`, a skip link, focus-visible styling). The problems are not taste problems. They are **art-direction and touch-ergonomics problems**, concentrated on mobile, plus a small number of outright functional bugs.

Five things are actively costing you conversions or credibility today:

1. **69% of the hero image is thrown away on a phone.** The hero is a landscape crop forced into a portrait hole.
2. **The vehicle detail hero is two-thirds empty black space on a phone.** `object-contain` in a 100vh box.
3. **The favourite button does not work on any vehicle card.** A stacking-context bug.
4. **Every form field zooms the page on iOS.** All six fields are 14px.
5. **Nothing in the site can be swiped.** Four carousels, zero touch handlers, 4px-tall dots as the only control.

None of these are cosmetic. Items 3–5 are defects; items 1–2 are the difference between "premium" and "template".

## 1. Hero section

### 1.1 Art direction is broken on mobile — the single highest-impact issue

**[VERIFIED]** Measured at 390×844:

| Slide | Source | Source ratio | Width needed to cover | Visible | **Cropped away** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `x70-plus-hero.png` 1920×1286 | 1.49 | 1260px | 390px | **69.0%** |
| 2 | `x50.jpg` 1772×1772 | 1.00 | 844px | 390px | **53.7%** |
| 3 | `x1.jpg` 1920×1286 | 1.49 | 1260px | 390px | **69.0%** |
| 4 | `t1.jpg` 1920×1286 | 1.49 | 1260px | 390px | **69.0%** |

The hero container is `h-screen` → 390×844, ratio **0.463**. The sources are 1.49 and 1.00. With `object-cover`, the browser scales to cover height, then discards the horizontal overflow. On slide 1 the browser renders a 1260px-wide image into a 390px window and throws away 870px of it.

**Why this matters (UX reasoning):** the hero is the only image a mobile user is guaranteed to see, and on a car site its single job is to communicate *this is a desirable vehicle*. A 31%-wide centre slice of a three-quarter-view SUV is not a vehicle — it's a door and a wheel arch. The user cannot assess proportion, stance, or silhouette, which is precisely what drives desire in the consideration phase. You are paying full bandwidth for the image and delivering a third of its communicative value.

**Second-order problem:** the crop is *inconsistent between slides*. Slide 2 is a 1:1 source (53.7% cropped), slides 1/3/4 are 1.49 (69% cropped). As the carousel advances every 6s, the apparent zoom level and framing jump. That reads as sloppy, and it is the kind of detail that separates Genesis/Volvo from a template.

**Fix:** dedicated portrait mobile hero assets via `<picture>` art direction (see §8). This is not a `sizes` problem — no `srcset` value can fix a wrong aspect ratio. **Impact: High.**

### 1.2 `h-screen` (100vh) is the wrong unit on mobile

**[VERIFIED]** `hero.tsx:62` uses `h-screen min-h-[560px]`; computed height 844px. No `dvh`/`svh` anywhere in site code (only in the unused shadcn `sidebar.tsx`).

On iOS Safari and Android Chrome, `100vh` is the *largest* viewport (toolbars hidden). With toolbars visible the hero is taller than the visible area, so the CTA and indicators sit below the fold on load, then shift as the toolbar collapses during scroll. Use `100svh` for the initial stable frame, or `100dvh` if you accept live resizing.

**Fix:** `min-h-[100svh]`. **Impact: Medium-High** (directly affects whether the primary CTA is visible on load).

### 1.3 No safe-area handling anywhere in the codebase

**[VERIFIED]** `grep -rn "safe-area\|env(safe" src/` → **zero matches.**

On notched/Dynamic-Island iPhones and Android devices with gesture bars, the bottom ~34px is reserved. Hero indicators sit at `bottom-10` (40px), which survives — but only by luck, and the pattern is absent site-wide, which is a latent risk for anything you later pin to an edge (see the floating CTA, §2.5).

**Fix:** add `env(safe-area-inset-*)` to bottom-pinned UI and set `viewport-fit=cover` deliberately. **Impact: Medium.**

### 1.4 Text overlay is tuned for desktop, not mobile

`hero.tsx:94–100` applies a **horizontal** gradient: `linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(0,0,0,0.62) 0%, ... transparent 68%)`. A left-to-right scrim is correct for a wide desktop canvas where text occupies the left third. On a 390px-wide screen, text spans the full width, so the right edge of the headline sits over the `transparent` end of the gradient. Contrast becomes image-dependent and therefore unpredictable — the site is relying on `textShadow` to rescue it.

**Fix:** vertical scrim on mobile (`bg-gradient-to-t from-black/80 via-black/30 to-transparent`), horizontal only at `lg:`. **Impact: Medium** (legibility + WCAG 1.4.3 predictability).

### 1.5 Visual hierarchy and conversion

- The eyebrow `TRAVEL+ SUV — ТАНЫ АДАЛ ЯВДАЛ ЭНДЭЭС ЭХЭЛНЭ` is 12px, `tracking-[0.18em]`, uppercase, Cyrillic, at 75% opacity. Wide letter-spacing on uppercase Cyrillic at 12px is materially harder to read than the Latin equivalent — Cyrillic lowercase has fewer distinguishing ascenders/descenders, so uppercase + tracking removes most word-shape cues. Either shorten it to 2–3 words or drop to `tracking-[0.12em]` and raise opacity to 90%.
- **No price anywhere in the hero.** For a mid-market SUV brand in this market, "from ₮X" is the single strongest qualifying signal and the mega menu already computes it (`priceOf`). Adding a from-price under the model name is the cheapest conversion win available. **Impact: High.**
- CTA sizing is actually fine — **[VERIFIED]** 212×48 and 201×46, both above the 44px minimum. Good.

### 1.6 Accessibility: autoplay cannot be paused on mobile — WCAG 2.2.2 failure

**[VERIFIED]** `hero.tsx:63–64` — pause is bound to `onMouseEnter`/`onMouseLeave` only. There is no touch or focus equivalent, and no visible pause control.

WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide, Level A) requires a mechanism to pause any auto-updating content that runs more than 5 seconds. The interval is 6s. On mobile there is **no such mechanism**, so this is a Level A failure, not a nice-to-have.

**Fix:** pause on `focus-within` and on touch, plus an explicit pause/play affordance; also pause when `document.hidden`. **Impact: High** (legal/compliance, and genuinely helpful for slower readers).

### 1.7 Hydration mismatch — real React error on the homepage

**[VERIFIED]** Console error on `/`:

```
A tree hydrated but some attributes of the server rendered HTML didn't match the client properties.
<div className="relative z-10 h-full flex flex-col justify-end pb-36 lg:pb-44"
+   style={{}}
-   style={{opacity:"1",transform:"none"}}
```

Cause: `hero.tsx:121` — `style={reduce ? undefined : { y: contentY, opacity: contentOpacity }}`. `useReducedMotion()` returns `null` during SSR and a boolean after mount, so the server and client render different `style` attributes. React explicitly **will not patch this up**.

**Fix:** gate on a mounted flag so the first client render matches SSR, then apply motion. **Impact: Medium** (console noise, wasted hydration work, and a real risk of a visible flash on the LCP element).

### 1.8 Global arrow-key listener

**[REVIEW]** `hero.tsx:44–52` attaches `keydown` to `window` for the lifetime of the homepage. Any ArrowLeft/ArrowRight press anywhere on `/` advances the carousel, including while a user is navigating a `<select>` or text field further down the page. Scope it to the carousel with `focus-within`, or check `e.target`.

## 2. Navigation

### 2.1 Model list is client-fetched — menu is empty on first open

**[VERIFIED]** `navbar.tsx:79–86` fetches `/api/public/models` in `useEffect`. The mega menu and the mobile drawer's model grid both render from that state.

Consequences: (a) on a cold, slow mobile connection a user who taps the hamburger immediately sees a drawer with **no models in it** — the primary navigation target of the entire site; (b) the model names and prices are invisible to crawlers in the nav; (c) it is an avoidable round-trip on every page load, since `layout`/`page` are already server components with `getAllCarModels()` available.

**Fix:** pass models from a server component into `<Navbar models={…} />`. Removes the request entirely and fixes the empty-state. **Impact: High.**

### 2.2 Touch targets throughout the nav and page

**[VERIFIED]** Sub-44×44px interactive elements: **37 on `/`, 42 on `/models/x70-plus`.** Worst offenders:

| Element | Measured | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Hero carousel dots | **14×4** and 32×4 | 44×44 |
| News carousel dots | **7×6**, 22×6 | 44×44 |
| Gallery dots (detail page) | **14×4** (×9) | 44×44 |
| Footer social icons | 20×20 | 44×44 |
| Footer nav links | 15×17 – 27×17 | 44×44 |
| Mobile menu button | 40×40 | 44×44 |

A 4px-tall target is not a target; it is a decoration that happens to be clickable. Apple HIG specifies 44×44pt, Material 48×48dp, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA, 2.2) requires 24×24 CSS px minimum — **the 4px and 6px dots fail even the relaxed WCAG floor.**

The fix does not require changing the visual design. Keep the 4px bar, and expand the hit area with a transparent pseudo-element or padding:

```css
.dot { position: relative; }
.dot::after {
  content: ""; position: absolute;
  left: 50%; top: 50%; transform: translate(-50%, -50%);
  width: 44px; height: 44px; /* invisible hit area */
}
```

**Impact: High.** This is the most common source of "this site feels cheap on my phone" without users being able to say why — mis-taps on a 4px target feel like the site is broken, not like the user missed.

### 2.3 Mobile drawer does not lock body scroll

**[VERIFIED]** `grep` for `document.body.style` / scroll-lock → **zero matches.** `navbar.tsx:368` renders the drawer with `max-h-[calc(100vh-4rem)] overflow-y-auto`.

With the drawer open, scrolling past its end chains to the page behind it. The user's scroll gesture starts moving the hero underneath the menu. This is disorienting and is the classic tell of an unfinished mobile menu.

Also note `max-h-[calc(100vh-4rem)]` repeats the `100vh` problem from §1.2 — on iOS the drawer can extend past the visible viewport.

**Fix:** lock `body { overflow: hidden }` (or `overscroll-behavior: contain`) while open; switch to `100svh`. Return focus to the trigger on close and trap focus inside the drawer while open. **Impact: Medium-High.**

### 2.4 Mobile drawer information architecture

The drawer leads with a 2-column grid of model cards — correct instinct, models are the primary intent. But:
- Model images are `aspect-[5/3]` `object-contain` at ~45vw ≈ 165px wide. At that size a whole SUV is ~40px tall; it is a smudge, not a recognisable vehicle. Either crop to a recognisable 3/4 front detail or drop images and use a clean text list with the from-price (which is already there and is the more useful decision input).
- Dropdown groups (`Бидний тухай`, `Худалдан авагчдад`) are rendered **fully expanded** as flat lists with a small uppercase label. That is defensible for 2–3 items, but combined with the model grid the drawer becomes a long scroll with weak grouping. Consider an accordion so the top level is scannable in one screen.
- `Тест драйв захиалах` is the last element, below the fold. The primary conversion action should be pinned or appear near the top.

**Impact: Medium.**

### 2.5 Sticky header and floating CTA

**[VERIFIED]** Header is `fixed … z-50` h-16 (64px) — reasonable. Floating CTA measured 56×56 at `bottom:80px, right:20px`, `z-50`.

The floating CTA meets the touch minimum. Two notes: it has no `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` (§1.3), and at `bottom-20` on mobile it will overlap the bottom of long content and any future sticky bar. Worth confirming it never covers form submit buttons on `/test-drive`.

### 2.6 Desktop nav

Solid: hover + click on the mega trigger, `aria-expanded`, `aria-haspopup`, outside-click and Escape handling, `aria-current` on active routes. Three gaps:
- The mega menu and dropdowns open on **hover** with no intent delay, so diagonal mouse movement across the nav flickers panels open/shut. Add ~120ms enter / ~250ms leave delay.
- Dropdown panels are not keyboard-navigable with arrow keys and do not trap/return focus; they are reachable by Tab only because they are in DOM order.
- `megaOpen` renders `grid-cols-3 sm:grid-cols-4 lg:grid-cols-6`, but the trigger only exists inside `hidden lg:flex` — the first two breakpoints are dead code.

## 3. Cards

### 3.1 Favourite button is completely non-functional — functional defect

**[VERIFIED]** Hit-tested all 10 cards on `/models` with `document.elementFromPoint()` at each button's centre:

```
{ size: "40x40", clickReachesButton: false,
  actualTopElement: "IMG object-cover transition-transform durati…",
  buttonZ: "auto" }        ← identical for all 10 cards
```

Cause: `vehicle-card.tsx:54–57` — the card's link overlay is `absolute inset-0 z-10`, while the favourite button at `vehicle-card.tsx:77–84` has **no z-index** (`z-auto`). The `z-10` link paints above it, so every tap on the heart navigates to the model page instead of favouriting.

The `useGarage` hook, the `aria-pressed` state, and the fill styling are all correctly implemented — the feature is finished and then covered by a transparent link. It cannot be triggered by any user on any card.

**Fix:** add `z-20` to the favourite button (and verify the compare button, which sits in the body and is unaffected). **Impact: High** — a shipped feature is 100% unreachable, and taps on it produce a confusing unwanted navigation.

### 3.2 Card image aspect ratio fights the source assets

**[VERIFIED]** On `/models`, card image box measured **365×228** (`aspect-[16/10]`), `object-cover`, source served from `x70-plus-poster-2.png` — a **1772×1772 square**.

A 1:1 source in a 16:10 window loses ~38% of its height. For a *poster* asset — framed deliberately as a square with the vehicle centred — that vertical crop lands on the vehicle body. Cards are the comparison surface; users scan them to shortlist. A cropped, inconsistently framed set of cards makes models look visually incoherent as a lineup.

**Fix:** export a dedicated 16:10 card crop per model (§8). **Impact: Medium-High.**

### 3.3 Card proportions and density

**[VERIFIED]** Card 367×**602**, `border-radius: 20px`, `border: 0.8px solid #E7E7EA`, `box-shadow: none`, body padding **24px**.

- 602px tall on an 844px viewport means **~1.4 cards per screen**. With `quick.slice(0, 6)` specs in a 2-column grid plus title, price, powertrains, and two buttons, each card is a spec sheet. On mobile that is too much: users in the browse phase want image + name + price + one differentiator, then tap through. Premium sites (Volvo, Genesis) keep listing cards deliberately sparse.
- **Recommendation:** show 2–3 specs on mobile (`quick.slice(0, isMobile ? 2 : 6)`), full set from `sm:` up. Target ~420px card height → ~2 cards per screen → materially faster lineup comprehension.
- `radius: 20px` on the card vs `rounded-full` buttons vs `rounded-xl` (12px) dropdowns vs `rounded-lg` (8px) detail-page CTAs — see §3.4.
- `box-shadow: none` at rest with `card-lift` on hover is a correct, restrained choice for a white premium aesthetic. Keep it. But note **`card-lift` hover has no mobile equivalent**, so on touch there is zero press feedback. Add an `:active` state (`scale(0.99)`) so taps feel acknowledged.

### 3.4 Radius and shadow inconsistency (site-wide)

Corner radii currently in use: `rounded-full` (buttons, hero CTAs), `20px` (cards), `rounded-2xl` (showcase), `rounded-xl` = 12px (dropdowns, textarea), `rounded-lg` = 8px (**detail-page hero CTAs**), `4px` (focus ring).

The detail-page hero uses `rounded-lg` CTAs (`model-detail-client.tsx:131,139`) while the homepage hero uses `rounded-full`. **The same action, in the same visual position, has two different shapes on two pages.** Pick one: `rounded-full` for pill actions everywhere, `rounded-2xl` for containers, `rounded-xl` for inputs.

Also **[VERIFIED]** a token bug: buttons are `#E20A17`, but their shadows are `rgba(226, 35, 26, …)` = `#E2231A` — a different red (`globals.css:310,316`). Invisible at low opacity, but it means two "brand reds" exist in the codebase. Unify.

**Impact: Low-Medium** individually, **High cumulatively** — radius/shadow drift is exactly what makes a site read as assembled rather than designed.

## 4. Vehicle pages (`/models/[id]`)

### 4.1 The detail hero is two-thirds empty on mobile — critical

**[VERIFIED]** At 390×844, measured on `/models/x70-plus`:

```
section box:  390 × 780   (h-[calc(100vh-4rem)])
image box:    390 × 780   objectFit: "contain"
rendered car: 390 × 262   (source ratio 1.49)
→ vehicle occupies 33.6% of hero height; 66.4% is empty #17181B
```

`model-detail-client.tsx:109` sets `className="object-contain"` on a `fill` image inside a 100vh-minus-header section. `contain` letterboxes: the car becomes a 262px-tall strip floating in a 780px black box, with the H1 and CTAs pinned to the bottom over dead space.

This is worse than the homepage crop. The homepage at least fills the screen; here the user's **first impression of the specific car they came to see** is a small strip of vehicle surrounded by void. On the product page — the highest-intent page on the site — this is the most damaging single issue in the audit.

**Fix (two options):**
- **Preferred:** portrait mobile hero asset + `object-cover` (§8). Full-bleed, deliberate composition.
- **Minimum:** if the source must stay landscape, drop the mobile hero to `h-[56svh]` and let it be a well-proportioned banner rather than a failed full-screen. A confident 56vh banner reads as premium; a 100vh box with a 262px car in it reads as broken.

**Impact: Critical.**

### 4.2 Galleries are not swipeable

**[VERIFIED]** `grep -rn "onTouchStart|onTouchMove|onTouchEnd|onPointerDown|drag"` across `model-detail-client.tsx`, `gallery.tsx`, `hero.tsx` → **zero matches.**

Measured on the detail page: multiple transform-based carousels (`flex h-full transition-transform duration-700`, `scrollWidth` up to 1952px inside a 390px window). Their only controls are the **14×4px** dots from §2.2.

So: the exterior gallery, interior gallery, and feature carousel — the heart of the vehicle page — cannot be swiped, and their alternative control is a 4px-tall dot. On mobile, horizontal swipe on an image strip is not a nice-to-have; it is the *expected* gesture. Users will swipe, nothing will happen, and they will conclude the gallery is broken and leave.

Note: `embla-carousel-react` is **already a dependency** (`package.json`). The tooling to fix this is installed and unused.

**Fix:** move these to Embla (drag + free-scroll + keyboard), or add native CSS scroll-snap (`overflow-x:auto; scroll-snap-type: x mandatory`) which is cheaper and gives momentum scrolling for free. **Impact: Critical.**

### 4.3 Specification layout

The quick-stat strip below the hero (`grid grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-4 divide-x divide-y`) is a good pattern — it is the Porsche/BMW "key figures" band, and it works.

Issues:
- `divide-x divide-y` on a 2-column mobile grid produces a visible cross of borders; with `px-2 py-7` and centred text the cells feel cramped horizontally at 183px wide. Consider dropping `divide-x` on mobile and relying on `gap` + generous `py`.
- The full spec table further down is the main reference surface. Verify it is grouped (engine / dimensions / capacity / safety) with sticky group headings rather than one long flat list — on mobile a 40-row flat table is unusable without grouping and collapse.

### 4.4 Heading hierarchy — an accessibility violation

**[VERIFIED]** Heading order on `/models/x70-plus`:

```
H1: JETOUR X70 Plus
H2: Гадна үзэмж
H2: Дотор салон
H2: Онцлох боломжууд
H2: JETOUR X70 Plus        ← duplicate of H1 text
H2: Техникийн үзүүлэлт
H2: JETOUR X70 Plus — мэдээлэл авах
H3: Санал хүсэлт
H2: Төстэй загварууд
```

The model name appears as H1 **and** twice more as H2. Screen-reader users navigating by heading hear "JETOUR X70 Plus" three times at two levels and cannot tell where they are. Make section H2s descriptive and unique (`Техникийн үзүүлэлт`, `Мэдээлэл авах`) and never repeat the H1 text as an H2.

**Impact: Medium** (WCAG 1.3.1 / 2.4.6).

### 4.5 Brochure download

**[VERIFIED]** Assets exist and serve correctly — `/brochures/x70-plus.pdf` → **200, application/pdf, 326KB**. All five PDFs present (t1, t2, x1, x50, x70-plus). Three separate brochure links render per page (`model-detail-client.tsx:497, 549, 588`).

Recommendations:
- Add `download` and surface the **file type and size** in the link text (`PDF · 319KB`). On mobile data, an unlabelled PDF link is a commitment users hesitate to make; labelling it measurably increases click-through and reduces bounce-back.
- Three identical brochure CTAs on one page dilutes each. Keep one in the spec section and one in the closing CTA block.
- Consider gating one variant behind the lead form later — but only after the ungated baseline is measured.

### 4.6 Comparison

`/compare` exists with a `compare-tray` and a 3-model cap, and the mega menu links to it. Good. Ensure the tray respects `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` and does not collide with the floating CTA at `z-50` (both are `fixed z-50` — verify stacking on a real device).

## 5. Landing pages & reading flow

### 5.1 Homepage section order

Current: `Hero → Models → News → Footer` (`app/page.tsx`).

For a distributor site whose goal is test-drive leads, News is the wrong second-to-last beat and there is no conversion block before the footer. Users who scroll past Models are your warmest traffic and they are handed a news list, then the footer.

**Recommended order:**
```
Hero → Models (lineup) → Why JETOUR / Brand proof → Financing teaser
     → Test-drive CTA block → News → Footer
```
`BrandStory` and `HomeHighlights` components already exist and are not used on the homepage — the content is built and unshipped.

**Impact: High** (adds a conversion surface where intent is highest).

### 5.2 Visual rhythm

`section-pad` (`clamp(4rem, 9vw, 7rem)`) and `section-pad-sm` (`clamp(3rem, 6vw, 5rem)`) is a good two-tier system, correctly fluid. Two refinements:

- At 390px, `9vw` = 35px, so `clamp` floors to **64px** top and bottom = 128px between sections. That is generous but acceptable for a premium feel. The real problem is that alternating white/`#F5F5F6` backgrounds are the only section delimiter; with 128px gaps and no other rhythm cue, long mobile scrolls lose orientation. Add a consistent eyebrow → H2 → lead triad at the top of every section (most sections already do this — enforce it everywhere).
- Vertical rhythm inside cards is inconsistent: `mb-1`, `mb-1.5`, `mb-6`, `mb-7`, `pt-6` in `vehicle-card.tsx` alone. Move to a 4/8px scale (`mb-1, mb-2, mb-4, mb-6, mb-8`) and stop using `1.5`/`3.5`/`2.5` steps decoratively.

## 6. Forms

### 6.1 Every field zooms the page on iOS — critical

**[VERIFIED]** On `/test-drive`, all six fields measured:

```
viewport meta: "width=device-width, initial-scale=1"   (no maximum-scale)

INPUT  text     fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true
INPUT  tel      fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true
SELECT          fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true
SELECT          fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true
INPUT  date     fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true
TEXTAREA        fontSize 14px   iosWillZoom: true

→ iosZoomFieldCount: 6 / 6
```

iOS Safari automatically zooms the viewport when a focused input's font-size is **below 16px**. Because there is no `maximum-scale` lock (correctly — locking it would be a WCAG 1.4.4 violation), the zoom **will** fire on every field.

What the user experiences: tap the name field → the page jumps and scales up → the layout is now wider than the screen → they must pinch back out or scroll horizontally to see the rest of the form → repeat on the next field. On a 6-field lead form this is the most abandonment-inducing bug on the site.

**Fix:** set form controls to `font-size: 16px` on mobile (`text-base` up to `sm:`, then `sm:text-sm` if you want 14px on desktop). One-line fix, disproportionate payoff. **Impact: Critical** — this sits directly on the primary conversion path.

### 6.2 No autofill support

**[VERIFIED]** `missingAutocomplete: 6/6`. No field has an `autocomplete` attribute, and none has a `name` attribute either.

Without `autocomplete="name"` / `"tel"` / `"email"`, iOS/Android will not offer contact autofill. Users type their name and phone by hand on a touch keyboard. Missing `name` attributes further defeat browser heuristics that might otherwise compensate.

WCAG 1.3.5 (Identify Input Purpose, AA) additionally *requires* `autocomplete` on fields collecting user information.

**Fix:** `autocomplete="name"`, `"tel"`, `"email"`; add `name` to every field; `inputMode="numeric"` on the phone field alongside `type="tel"`. **Impact: High** — cuts form completion time substantially, and reducing typing on mobile is the highest-leverage form optimisation that exists.

### 6.3 What is already right

`type="tel"` and `type="email"` are correctly used (`enhanced-lead-form.tsx:299,316`), which gives the right keyboards. Submit button measured **317×56 at 16px** — correctly sized. `type="date"` uses the native picker rather than a custom one — the right call on mobile.

### 6.4 Validation and field spacing

- Confirm validation is **inline and on blur**, not only on submit — the form uses `react-hook-form` + `zod`, so this is a configuration choice (`mode: "onBlur"`).
- Error text must not be conveyed by red colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1) — pair with an icon or explicit prefix.
- Field label/input pairs use a borderless `bg-transparent` input inside a bordered wrapper. That is elegant but reduces the perceived tap area; make the whole wrapper focusable-looking with `:focus-within` border colour change (verify this exists).
- Add `enterKeyHint="next"` / `"send"` to smooth multi-field keyboard flow.

## 7. Typography

### 7.1 The scale is genuinely good

`globals.css` defines a coherent fluid scale — `.type-display` (44→72px), `.type-h1` (36→56), `.type-h2` (32→48), `.type-h3` (22→28), `.type-lead` (17→18), `.type-small` (14). Single family (Inter), `text-wrap: balance` on headings, negative tracking that tightens as size grows, `line-height: 1.6` body. Italics are explicitly neutralised for Cyrillic. This is a considered system and should be preserved.

### 7.2 Cyrillic-specific issues

- **`.eyebrow` is 0.7rem (11.2px) with `letter-spacing: 0.22em`, uppercase.** This is the weakest typography in the system. Uppercase Cyrillic at 11px with 0.22em tracking is hard to read: it removes word shape, and Cyrillic uppercase forms (П/Н/И, Ш/Щ, Ь/Ъ/Ы) are more easily confused than Latin at small sizes. Raise to 12px and reduce tracking to 0.14em.
- The hero H1 uses `clamp(2.5rem, 5vw, 4rem)` — at 390px that is 40px for `JETOUR X70 Plus`, which fits. But longer future names (`JETOUR Dashing`, `JETOUR Traveller`) at `font-weight: 800` with `letter-spacing: -0.03em` will wrap awkwardly. Verify with the longest name in the lineup.
- `font-weight: 800` for all headings is heavy for Cyrillic, which has denser letterforms than Latin. Consider 700 for H2/H3 and reserve 800 for the hero display.

### 7.3 Contrast

Palette is well chosen: `#17181B` on white ≈ 15.9:1, body `#54585F` ≈ 7.2:1, muted `#6B7280` ≈ 4.8:1 — all pass AA, and the comment in `globals.css:47` shows this was checked deliberately.

**But `--muted-foreground: #8A8F98` (`:root`, line 85) is ≈ 2.9:1 on white — fails AA (4.5:1) for normal text.** It is a shadcn token, so it applies wherever `text-muted-foreground` is used. Audit those usages or raise the token to `#6B7280`.

Also **[REVIEW]** hero text on imagery relies on `textShadow` over a gradient whose right end is `transparent` (§1.4) — contrast there is image-dependent and cannot be guaranteed. Fix via the scrim.

### 7.4 Localisation quality

Copy is consistently Mongolian with correct terminology (`Хөдөлгүүр`, `Эргэлтийн хүч`, `Хурдны хайрцаг`, `Хөтлөгч`). `lang="mn"` is correctly set. Inter is loaded with the `cyrillic` subset — correct, and `display: "swap"` is right.

One gap: six weights (300–800) × 2 subsets is a large font payload. Audit which weights are actually used — if 300 and 500 are unused, dropping them reduces the critical font download.

## 8. Image strategy — the core question

### 8.1 The answer: neither A nor B alone. Use a hybrid, decided per section.

The question "one responsive image or two separate images" conflates two different mechanisms that solve two different problems. Getting this distinction right is the whole answer:

| | **Resolution switching** (`srcset` + `sizes`) | **Art direction** (`<picture>` + `<source media>`) |
|---|---|---|
| Solves | Same composition, different pixel density/size | **Different composition/crop per viewport** |
| Mechanism | Browser picks a size from one source | Author picks a *different file* per breakpoint |
| Next.js | `next/image` does this automatically | Requires `<picture>` or two conditionally-rendered `<Image>`s |
| Fixes wrong aspect ratio? | **No** | **Yes** |

`next/image` already gives you resolution switching for free. **No `sizes` value can fix the 69% crop in §1.1**, because the problem is the *shape* of the source, not its resolution. That requires art direction.

**So: use a single responsive image (A) everywhere the container has a fixed aspect ratio, and separate desktop/mobile assets (B) only where the container's aspect ratio changes dramatically between viewports.** That is exactly what Porsche, Volvo, Genesis, and Apple do — they do not ship two files for every image; they ship two only for full-bleed stages.

### 8.2 Where separate mobile images are genuinely required

| Section | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage hero** | **B — required** | Container goes 1.78 (desktop) → **0.463** (mobile). Measured 69% loss. |
| **Vehicle detail hero** | **B — required** | Measured 66.4% empty space. Highest-intent page on the site. |
| **Landing/campaign hero** | **B — required** | Same full-bleed geometry; usually contains layout-sensitive text. |
| **Promotional banners** | **B — required** | Contains typography baked into the image; text placement must be re-composed for portrait, and re-flowed text is unreadable when scaled. |
| **Gallery images** | **A — sufficient** | Fixed `aspect-[4/3]` or `16/10` container at all sizes. Only pixel count changes. |
| **Interior images** | **A — mostly** | Fixed ratio container. *Exception:* wide dashboard panoramas — consider a tighter mobile crop, since a 16:9 dashboard shot at 390px renders detail too small to read. |
| **Feature sections** | **A — sufficient** | Contained by a ratio box in a grid; the grid reflows, the image ratio does not. |
| **Vehicle cards / thumbnails** | **A — sufficient** | Fixed `aspect-[16/10]`. But **the source must match that ratio** — see §3.2, currently a 1:1 source. |
| **Menu thumbnails** | **A — sufficient** | Already correct: `800×480` (1.67) into `aspect-[5/3]` (1.67). Exact match. |

Note the menu thumbnails are the one place in the codebase where source ratio and container ratio already agree exactly, and they are WebP at 20–61KB. That is the standard to replicate.

### 8.4 Safe-composition guidelines for designers

The purpose of these rules is that **no crop at any viewport ever removes a defining feature of the vehicle.**

**Desktop hero (16:9):**
- Vehicle within the central **80%** horizontally, **75%** vertically.
- Anticipate the mobile crop: the centre **~31%** vertical band is what survives on a phone if the landscape asset is ever reused. Keep the vehicle's centre of mass there.
- Leave the left third (or the text side) free of high-frequency detail so the scrim reads cleanly.

**Mobile hero (9:16 portrait):**
- Compose *for portrait* — do not crop a landscape file. A three-quarter front view with the vehicle occupying the **lower 55–60%** and sky/environment above works best, because it leaves an uncluttered upper zone for the headline and a natural bottom for CTAs.
- **Reserve zones:** top **96px** (fixed 64px header + breathing room), bottom **200px** (headline + two CTAs + indicators + safe-area inset). Nothing critical inside these.
- Keep the vehicle's full width inside the central **85%** — the front wheel and mirror must never touch an edge.

**Universal:**
- **Never let a wheel, mirror, or badge be tangent to or clipped by a frame edge.** A clipped wheel arch is the single most obvious tell of an automated crop.
- Define one focal point per image and keep it out of the outer 10% on all sides.
- Where `object-cover` is unavoidable on a mismatched ratio, use `object-position` deliberately (e.g. `object-[50%_60%]` to bias toward the vehicle body) rather than accepting the default centre.
- Deliver a **safe-zone overlay PSD/Figma template** per asset type. This is what actually prevents regressions — guidelines in a document get forgotten, a template with visible reserve zones does not.

## 9. Performance

### 9.1 What is already correct

- `next.config.ts` sets `formats: ["image/avif", "image/webp"]` — AVIF first is the right order.
- `deviceSizes` is customised to `[360, 420, 640, 768, 1024, 1280, 1600, 1920]` — thoughtful, includes real small-phone widths.
- `priority` on the first hero slide and on the detail hero; `placeholder="blur"` with a shared `BLUR_DATA_URL`.
- `sizes` is present and mostly accurate (`vehicle-card.tsx:63` correctly declares `100vw / 50vw / 33vw`).
- `dns-prefetch` for Google Maps and Facebook, so the pixel doesn't contend with the LCP image.
- Fonts use `display: "swap"`, and ISR is set to 600s.

**[VERIFIED]** Only **1** hero image was fetched on initial load — `loading="lazy"` on slides 2–4 is working, so the four-image LCP contention I initially suspected does **not** occur. Good.

### 9.2 Source assets are heavier than they need to be

**[VERIFIED]** on disk:

```
x70-plus-hero.png          1920×1286   1,199 KB   PNG
x70-plus-poster-2.png      1772×1772   1,757 KB   PNG
x70-plus-detail-hero.png   2046×1370   1,180 KB   PNG
public/ total                             82 MB
```

PNG is the wrong container for photographic vehicle imagery — it is lossless, so you are storing photographic noise at full fidelity. `next/image` re-encodes to AVIF/WebP at request time, so the *delivered* bytes are fine, but you pay: larger repo, slower cold optimisation, more build I/O, and higher memory in `sharp`. Note the dev server already warned: `Slow filesystem detected`.

**Fix:** convert photographic PNGs to WebP/AVIF masters (keep PNG only where genuine transparency is needed — the `object-contain` studio shots). Expect roughly a 60–80% reduction in the 82MB tree.

### 9.3 Carousel slides beyond the first

Slides 2–4 lazy-load correctly, but each is a full-viewport image that downloads when the carousel auto-advances at 6s/12s/18s — whether or not the user is still on screen. On metered mobile data that is three unrequested full-bleed downloads.

**Fix:** prefetch only slide *n+1* on advance, and pause the timer when `document.hidden` (which also fixes part of §1.6).

### 9.4 The OG / structured-data image is broken — verified 404

**[VERIFIED]** by HTTP request:

```
/models-hero/x70-plus.png                          → 404  (text/html)
/_next/image?url=%2Fmodels-hero%2Fx70-plus.png…    → 400
/models-hero/x70-plus.jpg                          → 200  (498 KB)  ← the file that exists
```

`x70-plus.png` **does not exist on disk** (`ls` confirms only `.jpg`, plus `-hero.png`, `-detail-hero.png`, `-poster-2.png`). It is referenced in **four** places:

| Location | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `layout.tsx:54` — `openGraph.images` | **Facebook/Messenger shares render with no image** |
| `layout.tsx:65` — `twitter.images` | Twitter/X card has no image |
| `layout.tsx:77` — JSON-LD `AutoDealer.image` | Invalid structured data → weakens Google rich results |
| `brand-story.tsx:23` — on-page `<Image>` | **Broken image block on the homepage** |

Given Facebook and Instagram are the primary channels for this market (both linked in the JSON-LD `sameAs`), every shared link currently posts without a preview image. This is the highest-ROI single-line fix in the audit.

Also note the declared OG dimensions are `width: 2048, height: 2048` — square. OG images should be **1200×630 (1.91:1)**; a square image gets letterboxed or centre-cropped by every major platform.

**Fix:** create a purpose-built `og-default.jpg` at 1200×630 and point all four references at it. **Impact: High, effort: minutes.**

### 9.5 Other performance and build items

- **`typescript.ignoreBuildErrors: true`** (`next.config.ts:6`). Production builds ship even when types are broken. Given the hydration bug in §1.7 and the z-index bug in §3.1 both reached a running site, this setting is actively hiding the class of error you are experiencing. Turn it off and fix the fallout once.
- **Logos are raw `<img>` without dimensions** — `navbar.tsx:477,486` and `contact.tsx:207`. No `width`/`height` means no reserved space → CLS in the header, the most visible part of the page. Convert to `next/image` with explicit dimensions, or add `width`/`height` attributes.
- **`overflow-x: hidden` on `body`** (`globals.css:119`) masks horizontal overflow rather than fixing it, and can break `position: sticky` descendants. **[VERIFIED]** there is currently no overflow (`scrollWidth === clientWidth === 390` on all pages tested), so this can be removed safely — and then it will correctly *reveal* future regressions instead of hiding them.
- Consider `preload` for the detail-page hero via `priority` (already set) and confirm no `loading="eager"` images sit below the fold.

## Closing note

The gap between this site and a Volvo or Genesis site is **not** the design system — that part is already competent and in some respects better than typical. The gap is that the desktop composition was allowed to define the mobile experience: landscape images in portrait holes, hover states with no touch equivalent, 4px controls, and desktop-sized type in form fields.

Phase 1 is largely small, surgical fixes and removes every functional defect. Phase 2 — the portrait hero asset set — is the item that actually changes how premium the site feels, and it is a photography/export task more than an engineering one, so it can start in parallel today.
